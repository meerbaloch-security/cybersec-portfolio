## Analysis

**Evidence supporting malicious activity**

- Critical detection: Successful Logon After Brute Force.
- Activity spans 7 ATT&CK tactics: initial-access, execution, persistence, privilege-escalation, defense-evasion, credential-access, discovery.
- Credential-dumping behaviour after access was gained.
- Encoded PowerShell decodes to reconnaissance commands.
- Logon from a source IP never seen for this account.
- Impossible concurrency: j.smith was active on WS-JSMITH-01 inside the office (process started at 09:39) while the account logged in to SRV-RDS-01 from 198.51.100.77 at 09:42.

**Evidence against / benign explanations**

- No threat-intelligence match for any IP or domain involved.

**Enrichment and observations**

- SRV-RDS-01 is a high-criticality asset: Internet-facing Remote Desktop server.
- Decoded PowerShell on SRV-RDS-01: `whoami /groups; net localgroup administrators`
- First successful logon for j.smith from 198.51.100.77 in the available logs.

## Conclusion

**True positive**, high confidence (evidence score 13). The correlated evidence is consistent with an intrusion and is not explained by any known change or allowlist.

## Open questions and uncertainties

- How the password was obtained is unconfirmed: 25 guesses suggest a weak or reused password, not a full brute force.
- No outbound transfer from SRV-RDS-01 is logged after the SAM export, so it is unknown whether the hive (and its password hashes) left the server.

## Recommended actions

**Contain**

1. Disable or reset the password for j.smith, revoke all active sessions and tokens, and re-register MFA.
1. Block 198.51.100.77 at the perimeter firewall and VPN.
1. Isolate SRV-RDS-01 from the network (credential material was exposed).

**Eradicate**

1. Treat every local account on the host as compromised: rotate local administrator passwords (LAPS) and service-account secrets.
1. Remove the unauthorised member from the Administrators group and review other group changes by the same account.
1. Review PowerShell script-block logs (event 4104) on the host for any further commands.

**Recover**

1. Remove direct internet exposure of Remote Desktop: put it behind VPN or an RD Gateway with MFA.
1. Restore normal access for the affected user once the account is clean and MFA is enforced.

**Follow up**

1. Escalate to Tier 2 / incident response with this report; preserve logs and a memory image if the host is isolated.
1. Add the indicators of compromise to the threat-intel platform and hunt for them across all hosts.
